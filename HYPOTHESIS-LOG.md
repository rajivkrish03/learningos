## H-1 — 2026-10-27 (initial)

**Claim:** If an assistant decides how much to give based on what the learner can already do without it, then unaided performance after removal improves without today's task completion collapsing — because help withheld at the edge of ability forces the learner to retrieve rather than read, and retrieval is what makes a skill available later.

**Domain:** Process Content in Data Cloud (parsing, chunking, retrieval, evaluation).

**Mechanism assumed:** The help ladder (predict → MCQ → 3 rungs escalating from axis to worked example) withholds the answer until the learner has attempted retrieval. The model is that retrieval effort, not task completion, is what produces durable retention.

**What would move this:** A removal test showing Arm A (withholding) ≤ Arm B (answer-giving) on held-out items, or a ceiling failure showing items were too easy for either arm to show a difference.

**What we're explicitly NOT claiming yet:** That this works at scale, that the mechanism is retrieval specifically (vs. effort, time-on-task, or attention), or that the app's richer features (predict step, explain-back) are the active ingredient vs. the rung structure alone.

**Evidence so far:** None — study not yet run. This entry is the pre-study baseline.
